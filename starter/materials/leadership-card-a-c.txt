LEADERSHIP ACTIVITY — APPROVED LEARNING CARD A–C

A. Defined teaching situation
You are the instructor choosing how to run a 12-minute learning activity for 8 learners.

Observed learning problem from a prior synthetic exercise:
- 6 of 8 learners named a decision but did not support it with evidence.

Target learner output in the planned activity:
- one decision or choice;
- two relevant arguments connected to the available evidence or conditions;
- one limitation.

Your decision in this workshop is a TEACHING-FORMAT decision only:
- choose Format 1 or Format 2 for this defined teaching situation;
- explain why the chosen format fits the stated learning problem and conditions;
- identify one limitation of the chosen format.

You are NOT being asked to make, recommend, or evaluate any real operational or leadership decision.

B. Format 1 — explained instructor example
Timing:
- 3 minutes: instructor example;
- 6 minutes: learner writes own justification;
- 3 minutes: check.

Potential strength:
- may make it easier for learners to start.

Potential limitation:
- learners may copy the example instead of constructing their own reasoning.

C. Format 2 — guided dialogue
Timing:
- 3 minutes: learner writes own first attempt;
- 6 minutes: individual dialogue with up to three questions;
- 3 minutes: learner revises the answer.

Potential strength:
- keeps the learner's independent attempt visible and supports revision.

Potential limitation:
- requires the learner to engage with questions and may feel less direct than receiving an example.

Conditions and evidence you may use
Base your teaching-format decision only on the information in this card. You may use:
- the observation that 6 of 8 learners previously gave a decision without evidence;
- the 12-minute total time limit;
- the target learner output: one decision/choice, two arguments, one limitation;
- the timing of each format;
- the stated potential strength and limitation of each format;
- the fact that no additional reading is available during the activity;
- the rule that guided dialogue may use no more than three questions.

Do not invent additional learner characteristics, percentages, studies, citations, or evidence that are not in the card.

Both teaching formats may be acceptable if the justification connects the chosen format to the stated teaching situation and conditions and acknowledges a limitation.

The card contains no measured result showing that guided dialogue improves learning by 30% or by any other exact percentage.
